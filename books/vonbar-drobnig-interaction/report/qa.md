United Kingdom The law of vicarious liability for a delict/tort is identical in English law and Scots law. It arises simply because the party in law vicariously liable stands in a particular relationship with the person who committed the delict/tort in the context of that relationship. The principal examples of relationships capable of giving rise to it are those of employer to employee and partnership to partner. There is neither a requirement to prove any form of fault on the part of the person vicariously liable, nor is there a defence that there was no fault on the part of that person. The liability has been developed as additional to the liability of the person who committed the delict/tort.
