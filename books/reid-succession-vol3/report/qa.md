7. Protection of the forced share
(a) General
As in the case of other countries which guarantee close family members a forced share or a compulsory portion,249 the South American jurisdictions try to ensure that the protection they seek to provide is not thwarted or hollowed out by voluntary or involuntary acts of the future deceased. Not only might he or she make testamentary dispositions that challenge the distribution envisaged by the law, but transfers of assets may take place during lifetime so that, at the moment of death, very little or nothing is left in the estate. Besides, testators might try to encumber the forced share with charges or set out other limitations, or attempt to convince forced heirs to renounce their future legítima.
