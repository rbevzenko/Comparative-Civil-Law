Fitness for Habitation: Leases
In general, a landlord gives no implied undertaking that leased premises are or will be fit for habitation or
for any particular use,148 or that the premises can lawfully be used for any particular purpose.149 But
where a house or flat is let furnished, there is an implied covenant or warranty that it is reasonably fit for
human habitation when let,150 although there is no obligation at common law to keep furnished or
unfurnished premises in that condition or to repair them during the tenancy.151 However, covenants on the
part of the landlord are implied in the cases of houses let at a low rent152 or for a short term.153

Benefit
There is old authority for the view that the underlying principle is one of benefit to the minor-that is, if the
contract were beneficial to the minor, he could not avoid it at all,177 whereas if it were not beneficial, he
was not bound at all.178 But since the mid-nineteenth century it has been established that even if the
contract is not beneficial, the minor is bound if he takes possession of the property, but only until he
disclaims within the time stated.179

Time of Tender
Where by the terms of the contract the money is to be paid on a particular day, the tender to be valid must be
made on that day.488 Although a tender cannot be effectually made after the actual commencement of an
action for the recovery of the debt, that is, after the issue of the writ,489 nevertheless, before the issue of a
writ, a late tender of the amount due will normally have the same effect, in regard to interest and costs,490
as would a valid tender on the due day.491 Where a charterparty gave the owner an express power to
withdraw the vessel on default in payment of hire, late payment made before the owner exercised its right to
withdraw was held not to deprive the owner of that right.492

> [488] Dixon v Clark (1848) 5 C.B. 365, 378–379.
